# Where I End

The line between you and not-you feels obvious and is actually drawn, and
re-drawn, by the brain. A tool becomes part of your body schema; a rubber
hand can feel like yours; the self has an editable border.

Make a soft creature the player *is* — a blob, a field, a cloud of sound
— with a boundary that can grow, shrink, and reach. Touch something and
it might become you (absorbed inside the border) or stay other. As the
self expands, so does what it feels: your heartbeat, rendered in sound,
spreads into whatever you've made part of you, so you literally *hear* the
edge of yourself move. Lose a part and its beat drops out.

Find the game in the boundary: maybe you must grow to survive but each
absorption changes what "you" wants, or you must decide what to let go.
The felt question is where the player stops and the world starts — and how
movable that turns out to be.

## Constraints
- Playable in seconds: touch, absorb, feel yourself change size. No
  manual.
- Sound is the self-signal — the heartbeat marks the boundary; audio off
  loses the body. Web Audio, local; no CDN.
- Mobile-web first: portrait, touch, opens cold in a phone browser, no
  build, no server, no login. This page is its own door.
- One self-contained page under `growth/`; vendor any assets.

## Bloom looks like
- The player feels the border of "me" move under their thumb — and
  hesitates before absorbing the next thing.
