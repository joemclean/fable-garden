# Seeing With Your Ears

The brain doesn't much care which nerve the data comes down — feed it the
right structure through the ears and it will build something like sight.
Sensory-substitution devices let blind users "see" by turning images into
soundscapes. It's uncanny how fast it starts to feel like space.

Build a tiny world you navigate by sound alone. The screen is dark or
near-dark; the scene is painted only in audio — height mapped to pitch,
left/right to stereo pan, nearness or brightness to loudness or timbre.
The player learns to hear where things are and move toward or away from
them: find the exit, avoid the thing, follow the tone. Start dead simple
(one sounding target in the black) and let complexity grow as the ear
learns.

The point is the moment the sound stops being noise and becomes a place.
Give the player that moment early, then deepen it. This is the most
audio-native seed in the garden — lean all the way in.

## Constraints
- Playable in seconds: move toward the sound. No manual, minimal text.
- Sound is the entire display — vision is deliberately withheld. Web
  Audio, local; no CDN.
- Mobile-web first: portrait, touch, opens cold in a phone browser, no
  build, no server, no login. Headphones help — say so gently in-world.
- One self-contained page under `growth/`; vendor any assets.

## Bloom looks like
- A player closes their eyes, hears the room, and reaches the target —
  and it felt like seeing.
