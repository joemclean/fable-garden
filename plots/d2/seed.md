# What Red Sounds Like

The hard problem: why is there something it is *like* to see red? You can
describe wavelengths forever and never touch the redness itself. Qualia
are private, and no one can be sure their inner colors match yours.

Make a game about that gap. Give the player a private inner quale — say a
specific color-tone, felt but not nameable — and ask them to convey it to
another mind (the machine, or a second player) using only crude shared
controls: a few sliders, a rhythm, a gesture. The other mind guesses.
Neither can ever check the inside; they can only converge on behavior.
The play is the doomed, tender effort of translation, and how close you
can get across a gap that can't fully close.

Sound is the natural medium for the untranslatable here — a tone-color
has qualia too, and mapping seen-color to heard-tone (or the reverse)
stages the synesthetic leap the whole problem is about. Let the
near-misses be poignant, not just wrong.

## Constraints
- Playable in seconds: try to send your inner thing across. No manual.
- Sound is the currency of the private quale and its translation. With
  audio off, half the language is gone. Web Audio, local; no CDN.
- Mobile-web first: portrait, touch, opens cold in a phone browser, no
  build, no server, no login. This page is its own door.
- One self-contained page under `growth/`; vendor any assets.

## Bloom looks like
- Two minds get *close* to sharing an inner thing and feel exactly where
  the gap stays open. The near-miss is the art.
