# Didn't You Notice

We assume we'd see a big change happen right in front of us. Often we
don't — if the moment of change is masked, even a huge alteration slides
by. The world feels continuously, completely seen; it isn't.

Build the classic flicker made playful: a scene, a blank (or a flash, or
a sound), then the scene again with one thing different — a color, a
missing object, a moved shape. The player hunts the change by tapping
where they think it happened. As it goes on, make the misses instructive
— near-identical decoys, changes in the very thing you're most sure
you're watching.

The audio does the masking work a blink or a saccade does in life: a
sound on each flip that momentarily swallows the change, so the player
feels *why* they missed it, not just that they did. Consider a mode where
the change is only in the sound — a note that vanished from a chord.

## Constraints
- Playable in seconds: look, tap the change. No manual.
- Sound is integral — it masks the change and can *be* the change. With
  audio off the core illusion is gone. Web Audio, local; no CDN.
- Mobile-web first: portrait, touch, opens cold in a phone browser, no
  build, no server, no login. This page is its own door.
- One self-contained page under `growth/`; vendor any assets.

## Bloom looks like
- The player stares dead at a spot, misses the change anyway, and laughs
  — the gap between feeling all-seeing and being it, in their thumb.
