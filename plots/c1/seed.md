# One Thing

Your brain builds a unified object out of separately-processed features
— this color, that motion, this pitch — and hands you a single seamless
thing. It's called the binding problem, and it only becomes visible when
binding fails.

Make a piece where an object comes apart into its features and the
player's job is to put it back together. A shape, its color, and a tone
each drift onto their own timeline — slightly out of phase, then wildly.
The player nudges them (drag, tap, tilt) back toward sync; when color,
motion, and sound line up, they *snap* into one perceived thing, and the
snap should feel unmistakable, almost bodily.

Audio is one of the features being bound, equal to the visual ones — not
a reward sound layered on top. The interesting zone is the *almost*: when
things are close but not bound, and the player feels the two readings
flickering. Let the difficulty come from more features, or features that
mislead.

## Constraints
- Playable in seconds: nudge things until they become one. No manual.
- Sound is a feature to be bound, integral to the mechanic. With audio
  off, a third of the object is missing. Web Audio, local; no CDN.
- Mobile-web first: portrait, touch, opens cold in a phone browser, no
  build, no server, no login. This page is its own door.
- One self-contained page under `growth/`; vendor any assets.

## Bloom looks like
- That snap — separate streams becoming one object — lands in the body,
  and the player chases it again.
