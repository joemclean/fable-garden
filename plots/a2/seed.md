# Trust the Feeling

In blindsight, people with certain brain damage can't consciously see a
thing — yet forced to guess where it is, they're right far more often
than chance. Knowing without the feeling of knowing; acting on
information that never reaches awareness.

Make a game the player can only win by trusting a signal they can't
justify. The target is hidden — no visible cue, or one too faint to
consciously use. But a sound quietly tracks the truth: a tone that warms
as they near it, a texture that shifts, a pull felt before it's known.
Scoring rewards committing to the hunch and punishes waiting for
certainty that never comes.

The design challenge is to make "I don't know why I did that, but I was
right" a repeatable, trainable feeling. Consider withholding all feedback
about *why* they succeeded, so success stays a little spooky.

## Constraints
- Playable in seconds: act on the feeling, find out you were right. No
  manual.
- Sound carries the sub-conscious signal — it is the whole mechanism.
  With audio off there's nothing to trust. Web Audio, local; no CDN.
- Mobile-web first: portrait, touch, opens cold in a phone browser, no
  build, no server, no login. This page is its own door.
- One self-contained page under `growth/`; vendor any assets.

## Bloom looks like
- The player commits blind, is right, and can't say why — and comes back
  to feel it again.
