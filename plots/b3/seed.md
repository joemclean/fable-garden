# Two Minds

Cut the bridge between the hemispheres and you get two centers of
experience in one skull — each seeing half the world, each unable to say
what the other knows, sometimes literally working at cross-purposes.
Consciousness turns out to be divisible.

Build a two-thumbs game where each hand is a hemisphere. Split the screen
and the stereo field: the left thumb sees and hears only its side, the
right only its. Neither half is told what the other half knows, and the
task can only be solved by both — but they can't communicate, only act
and infer. Let the player feel the strange strain of being two who must
cooperate as if one, and the small comedy when the hands disagree.

Audio is hard-panned so each ear feeds its hemisphere; a cue on the left
never reaches the right's knowledge except through what the left *does*.
The uncanny target: a moment where the player's two hands seem to hold
different beliefs.

## Constraints
- Playable in seconds: two thumbs, two halves, one task. Minimal manual.
- Sound is split L/R as the two channels of divided knowledge — integral.
  Web Audio, local; no CDN. Headphones deepen it; say so gently.
- Mobile-web first: portrait, two-thumb touch, opens cold in a phone
  browser, no build, no server, no login. This page is its own door.
- One self-contained page under `growth/`; vendor any assets.

## Bloom looks like
- The player's two hands seem, for a second, to be two minds — and it's
  funny and unsettling at once.
