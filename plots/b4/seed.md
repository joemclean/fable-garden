# Did I Do That?

The sense of agency — that *I* caused this — is a feeling the brain
constructs, and it can be fooled. Nudge the timing between an intention
and an effect and people will claim actions that weren't theirs, or
disown ones that were. Free will's signature is editable.

Build a game that plays with authorship. Sometimes the player's tap moves
the dot; sometimes the dot moves on its own and a well-timed click makes
them *feel* they did it; sometimes their real action is delayed until it
feels like someone else's. Feedback probes the illusion: ask "did you do
that?" and reveal how often the feeling lied. Delayed audio-clicks are the
lever — the brain binds a sound just after an action into a sense of
cause.

The uncanny target is the double failure: claiming an act you didn't
commit, and disowning one you did — both in the same session, both
convincing. Keep it honest and a little unsettling.

## Constraints
- Playable in seconds: act, and feel (or doubt) that it was you. No
  manual.
- Sound-timing is the mechanism of the agency illusion — integral, not
  feedback candy. Web Audio, local; no CDN.
- Mobile-web first: portrait, touch, opens cold in a phone browser, no
  build, no server, no login. This page is its own door.
- One self-contained page under `growth/`; vendor any assets.

## Bloom looks like
- The player confidently claims an act that wasn't theirs — and feels the
  sense of "I did it" come loose.
