# The River

William James called it a stream — thought isn't a row of discrete items
but a current, always moving, never the same water twice, and you can only
stand in one part of it at a time. Try to hold a thought still and it's
already gone downstream, replaced.

Make the stream visible and audible and let the player stand in it. A
continuous current of half-formed thoughts drifts past — fragments of
text, murmured audio, images half-surfacing — too many to hold. The
player can attend to exactly *one* at a time: catch it, and it clarifies
and speaks while the rest wash by unheard; let go, and it dissolves back
into the flow. There's no pausing the river. The play is in choosing what
to hold and grieving, lightly, everything you let pass.

Audio is the murmur of the stream — many voices at the edge of sense, one
resolving clear when attended. This one can be more poem than puzzle; let
it be beautiful and a little melancholy.

## Constraints
- Playable in seconds: touch a thought to hold it, release to let it go.
  No manual.
- Sound is the stream itself — a murmur that clarifies on attention. Audio
  off silences it. Web Audio, local; no CDN.
- Mobile-web first: portrait, touch, opens cold in a phone browser, no
  build, no server, no login. This page is its own door.
- One self-contained page under `growth/`; vendor any assets.

## Bloom looks like
- The player feels the current — the impossibility of holding it all, the
  small ache of what washed past — and finds it beautiful.
